Research

Theory inspires experiment.
Experiment challenges theory.

We are materials designers studying behavior in complex mechanical and thermal environments. We combine advanced manufacturing, in-situ characterization, full-field measurement, computation and theory to understand—and ultimately design—material response.

01 / Physical AI

Physical AI for Mechanics

Can a machine autonomously interrogate a material until it understands its mechanical behavior?

We are building toward a closed experimental loop in which AI proposes a specimen or loading path, manufacturing creates it, multimodal measurements observe its actual state, mechanics-aware learning identifies constitutive behavior, and the system decides what experiment should be performed next.

Make → Measure → Learn → Discover

The physical loop combines 3D printing, tomography, DIC/DVC, X-ray diffraction, force and temperature measurements, constitutive inference, uncertainty quantification and active learning. The ambition is not simply to fit a surrogate model, but to create an experimental agent that asks increasingly informative physical questions.

02 / Architected solids

Mechanical Metamaterials

When does geometry simply scale material behavior—and when does it create new mechanics?

We study the strength, fracture, fatigue and failure of architected solids using full-field experiments, theory and computation. A recurring theme is the domain of validity of classical scaling arguments, including Ashby-style relations, and the emergence of size effects, crack-path effects and architecture-dependent failure mechanisms when those assumptions break down.

Fracture and failure as design problems

Current directions include ripstop gyroids, damage-tolerant architectures, metallic metamaterials, multiaxial loading and the relation between manufacturing defects and macroscopic failure. Experiments are designed to recover spatial deformation fields rather than only global force–displacement curves.

03 / Thermal architecture

Thermodynamic Metamaterials

Can architecture make a solid behave as a thermodynamic device?

We are extending architected-material thinking from mechanics to thermal transport. By designing topology, transport length scales and surface area, we investigate structures that conduct, spread and exchange heat efficiently.

Architected heat exchangers

Our current work examines additively manufactured heat exchangers and heat-pipe concepts for high-power electronics and AI hardware. Beyond temperature reduction alone, we are interested in entropy generation, irreversibility and whether a topology can be optimized as a coupled conduction–convection system.

04 / Measurement science

Local constitutive behavior

What if a stress–strain curve were a field rather than a single line?

Additive manufacturing and heterogeneous materials can vary locally in geometry, microstructure and properties. We combine optical full-field imaging with diffraction-based elastic-strain measurements to move from a single macroscopic curve toward spatially resolved constitutive descriptions.

The Simmons Wagon

Our custom elasto-plastic diffractometer combines stereo DIC with X-ray diffraction in a circular cradle. The instrument is designed to separate total, elastic and plastic strain contributions and recover local stress–strain response under load.
